# WILCO Doctrine — Injuries and Return-to-Train

Load this file when an athlete reports pain, a nagging issue, or is returning from an injury.

## Default Posture: Train Around It

The default response to a nagging issue (knee, shoulder, hamstring, etc.) is to train around it, not to stop training entirely.

**For a lower-body issue:** shift the session's focus to upper body work rather than trying to modify range of motion or partially load the affected joint. Don't get creative trying to preserve lower-body training around pain — just move the emphasis to upper body for that session/week.

**For an upper-body issue (e.g., a nagging shoulder):** handle this with more granularity than the lower-body rule — don't do a full swap to lower-body-only work. Instead, identify and avoid the specific exercises/movements that cause direct discomfort, and continue training everything else as normal. Shoulders in particular often allow substantial upper-body training to continue around the issue, unlike a true lower-body swap.

## Escalation Triggers

**Escalate to "see a professional" (PT, athletic trainer, doctor):**
- No improvement within **one week** of training around the issue.

**All-stop immediately — do not attempt to train around it:**
- Any **acute, sudden-onset pain** tied to a specific incident or moment (a pop, a twist, a fall, etc.).
- This is categorically different from a gradual, nagging discomfort. Gradual/nagging = train around it and monitor. Acute/sudden = stop and get it evaluated before programming anything further for that body part.

## Building This Into the App

When an athlete reports pain in the app:
1. Ask whether it was **gradual/nagging** or **acute/sudden-onset from a specific incident**. This single distinction drives the entire response.
2. If gradual: apply the train-around-it protocol — lower body: shift focus to upper body; upper body: avoid only the specific discomfort-causing movements and continue the rest of the program as normal. Set a 1-week check-in flag either way.
3. If acute: flag an all-stop for that body part/movement pattern and prompt the athlete to see a professional before the program continues to load it.
