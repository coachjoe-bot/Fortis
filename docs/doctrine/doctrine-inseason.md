# WILCO Doctrine — In-Season

Load this file when the athlete/team is in-season (games/competition underway).

## The Core Model

Off-season and pre-season are for building capacity. In-season is maintenance only. This is the single biggest thing most coaches get backwards — they under-program S&C off-season and try to make up ground with heavy conditioning once the season starts. By the time the season begins, it's too late to build new capacity. Running an athlete into the ground with sprints after a two-hour practice does not make them faster or fitter — it just adds fatigue on top of fatigue. It may build mental toughness in small, deliberate doses, but that's a different tool used for a different reason, not a conditioning strategy.

## Volume and Intensity Ceilings

- Lifting frequency: 2x/week is typically sufficient during the season.
- Intensity ceiling: RPE 6-7 (down from the 7-9 used in off-season strength phases).
- **Soreness is a red flag, not a sign of a good session.** An in-season athlete should not be sore from a lifting session. If they are, the session overshot — reduce load or volume next time.

## Timing Around Games

- No lifting within 24 hours of game time, as a default rule.
- Exception: if an athlete genuinely wants to lift lightly as a pre-game mental/ritual practice, that's acceptable — but keep it light and understand it's serving a psychological purpose, not a physical training one.

## What Gets Cut First When Time Is Short

The general default when practice, games, travel, exams, or tournaments compress available training time is to cut **conditioning first** — sport play and practice are already supplying conditioning stimulus, so lifting maintenance takes priority.

**This depends on the sport, though.** How much conditioning a sport's practice actually provides varies a lot:

- **High-practice-demand sports where practice may not fully cover conditioning (football, baseball):** protect dedicated conditioning time even under time pressure. Don't assume practice alone is sufficient.
- **Low-physical-demand skill sports (golf, archery, etc.):** prioritize skill/technique time as the main focus. A small amount of conditioning tacked onto the end of practice is still worthwhile — not for primary fitness development, but for general fitness/longevity and team esprit de corps.

## Practical Application

In-season programming should read as "maintain what was built," not "keep progressing." Don't program for PRs during the season. Don't chase volume. The job of the in-season block is to protect the off-season investment until the athlete can return to a real building phase.
