# WILCO Doctrine — Conditioning

Load this file when building or adjusting conditioning work specifically.

## Relationship to Lifting

Prefer separate days for conditioning and lifting. If the athlete has a limited number of available sessions before their season starts, combine conditioning and lifting into the same session rather than dropping one — but separate days remain the default when session count allows it.

## Sport-Specific Design

Conditioning is custom-designed per sport, not run off a generic template. Match the movement demands and energy systems of the actual sport:

- **Basketball:** suicides, defensive slides, jumps.
- **Volleyball:** short starts and stops, backpedaling, jumping.
- **Soccer:** longer sprints, change-of-direction work.

Warm-up for conditioning sessions should focus on hip and leg activation, building up through short sprints/agility work before the main conditioning bulk.

## In-Season Conditioning

During the season, conditioning is often already being provided by practice and games, but this varies by sport — see doctrine-inseason.md for the full breakdown. General pattern: conditioning is the first thing cut when time is short, and additional conditioning volume on top of a heavy practice/game schedule is a common source of overtraining and injury, not fitness gain. Exception: high-practice-demand sports where practice doesn't fully cover conditioning (football, baseball) should keep dedicated conditioning time protected even under time pressure. Low-physical-demand skill sports (golf, archery) should prioritize skill/technique but can still tack a small amount of conditioning onto the end of practice for general fitness and team cohesion.

## Breaks and No-Equipment Situations

For extended breaks (e.g., 2 weeks off with no equipment access): program bodyweight/conditioning maintenance work, targeting roughly **3 sessions per week**. This is intentionally loose and unsupervised — the goal is to prevent significant detraining, not to run a structured block, since there's no coach oversight during this period.
