# WILCO Doctrine — Research List

Methodologies and sources named by Coach Joe, with his commentary. Ordered roughly by how central he'd want each to be to WILCO's programming logic. Each entry needs deeper independent research beyond Joe's summary — his commentary defines what to look for and what to watch out for.

## 1. Starting Strength (Mark Rippetoe)
**Joe's take:** "The absolute foundation for strength training. Simple, straightforward, and effective." No misapplication or caveat noted — treat as the base-layer reference for novice linear progression.

## 2. 5x5 (general/Bill Starr-style)
**Joe's take:** Similar role to Starting Strength — a great starting point for young athletes who just want to get strong, using linear progression. Good fit for newer lifters who haven't yet hit a plateau.
**Misapplication:** People tend to tack on excessive accessory work, because the base program feels "too simple" on its own. Watch for programs that pad a 5x5 base with unnecessary volume.

## 3. Wendler 5/3/1
**Joe's take:** Best suited for more advanced lifters who've already exhausted their "newbie gains" and need a structured reset. Also works well as a peaking program for elite lifters heading into a meet.
**Use case for WILCO:** advanced-athlete reset phases, and peaking blocks ahead of a defined competition/testing date.

## 4. Westside Barbell / Conjugate Method (Louie Simmons)
**Joe's take:** Very advanced system — reserve for high-end athletes only, not general programming. That said, Joe pulls **banded variations** from it into the power phase when equipment is available.
**Misapplication:** Athletes go way too heavy on dynamic-effort days, which defeats the speed-development purpose of the day and raises injury risk. If building conjugate-style dynamic effort work into the app, cap intensity guidance hard and explain why.

## 5. Knees Over Toes Guy (Ben Patrick / ATG)
**Joe's take:** Great for out-of-the-box unilateral movement work and building tendon capability. Strong tool for improving jumping mechanics and knee health.
**Misapplication:** People add it on top of an existing program without adjusting total volume, and end up overtrained. If pulling KOT-style work into a program, treat it as a volume addition that requires offsetting cuts elsewhere, not a free add-on.

## 6. CrossFit Methodology
**Joe's take:** Values the group-training mentality and the high-intensity metcon framework — traces back to military-style PT designed to train 20-40 people at once and get everyone pushing themselves.
**Misapplication:** Because it's fun and competitive, it's easy for athletes to overtrain or push too hard. Requires active coach control during the workout to rein people in — it's not a "set it and let the clock run" system.

## 7. Renaissance Periodization / Dr. Mike Israetel (RP)
**Joe's take:** Excellent, scientifically grounded explanation of biomechanics and hypertrophy programming.
**Caveats:**
- Primarily hypertrophy-focused, which is somewhat one-sided if the athlete's goal is athletic performance rather than muscle growth.
- Content is not age-appropriate for younger audiences — be careful sourcing RP material directly for a youth-facing app.
**Misapplication:** People assume hypertrophy is the goal of all training, when it's one tool among several depending on the athlete's actual objective.

## 8. Squat University (Dr. Aaron Horschig)
**Joe's take:** Excellent for biomechanics explanations and cueing to work around nagging injuries. PT background means strong recovery advice and lifting-mechanic fixes.
**Use case for WILCO:** good source for the injury/return-to-train doctrine (see doctrine-return.md) — cueing fixes for common lifting-mechanic faults tied to nagging pain.

## The One Cross-Cutting Guardrail Joe Wants Built In
Most coaches get the season model backwards: they under-program strength & conditioning in the off-season and over-emphasize conditioning once the season starts — by which point it's too late to build real capacity. Real development happens off-season and pre-season; in-season is maintenance only. This should function as a guardrail across the entire app, not just a note in the in-season file — see doctrine-core.md and doctrine-inseason.md.
