1 A Rec has to earn its place

First mention: noted. Pattern: Rec.

No more drafting a change every time a log looks different or pain gets mentioned once. The first mention becomes a watched note in the AI's memory — Joe says so and moves on. A Rec only gets drafted when one of three bars is cleared:

1. The same or similar issue shows up again within ~two weeks (a pattern).
2. The first report is clearly serious — sharp pain, couldn't finish the lift.
3. You clearly ask for a change yourself.

Circumstances shift. One odd day is allowed to be one odd day — the watched note expires quietly if nothing recurs.

2 The Rec sheet, rev 2

Week-tagged lines, WHY out of the way, scope by talking.

Every replaced line names its week and day. Same exercise in the same slot across two weeks — like this recovery bench in weeks 2 and 3 — is never ambiguous about which one is moving.

WHY is a collapsible strip at the top (shown open here; it collapses to one line after you've read it), so the edits get the room.

The scope section is gone. Want more or less in the Rec? Say it: "I also want to change the dips" or "leave week 3 alone" in the composer, and the swap list re-draws. Chips were too limiting — Joe isn't.

How long is concrete: rest of block, a hard number of weeks, or permanent. Nothing vague that can't be judged, and a timed Rec reverts itself when the clock runs out.

Tap any replacement to hand-edit it — your text applies verbatim. Joe only rewrites a hand-edited line if you explicitly tell him to, and even then only that line.

3 Your log becomes your program

"Make what I logged my new Monday — and progress it."

The ask that triggered this one: "Make the workout I logged on 8/22 my new Monday for this block, and make sure I'm progressing — compounds up a percent each week, accessories just a little more than the week before."

The mastermind reads the actual log — exercises, sets, reps, loads — and drafts every Monday of the block: the compound climbs on real percentages off your 335 front squat max (76 → 80 → 85%), the accessories nudge by small loads or reps. Week by week, spelled out, editable per line.

"Make the workout I just logged / last week's / the one on 8/22…" all resolve the same way — the log is addressable by date or recency.

Surgical by construction: only the Monday lines are touched. Snatch pull and back extensions leave because they're part of the Monday being replaced — nothing outside those lines moves, ever.

5 The Proof Feed asks first

Flag → permission → parked draft.

When the weekly review surfaces something Rec-worthy — a stall, a recurring complaint — it never drafts on its own. It asks. Say yes and the Rec is drafted and parked straight to Past Blocks, not popped open: the check-in ends clean, and the draft waits for you. Say no and nothing is created.

The check-in's old auto-generated apply card is gone entirely. Same pattern-rule discipline applies here too: the review proposes drafting only for things that have repeated, not one-week blips.

6 One drawer, clear lifetimes

Recs live in Past Blocks until deleted or their block retires.

Parked Recs, applied-with-a-clock Recs, Builder drafts, paused interviews and closed blocks all live here. Reopen a parked Rec and the sheet comes back exactly as it was. Delete it and it's gone.

Only one Rec bar is ever live on screen; anything new lands here with a one-line mention from Joe. When the block a Rec references is retired, its Recs retire with it.

My rec: yes — one bar for every auto-flag. Your explicit asks always skip the bar.
